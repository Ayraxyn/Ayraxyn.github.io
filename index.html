Part 1: Introduction

Firstly, there are more types of felting than just knitting! Some of these other forms include but is not limited to Crochet Art, Macrame, Spinning, and felting! Which will be main focus of this website. However, among all the fiber art types, they all achieve the same goal within the same medium - as the name suggests, all these art forms use fabric, wool and felt like materials! Most of these mediums also involve the use of a needle or an object able to weave between the fiber of these materials, so if you. planning to start your fiber art journey, do get used to poking yourself!

Part 2: The First Thread

The oldest form of fiber art also happens to be the type we will be discussing today! Felting was theorized to first have been started in 6000BCE in Antolia, however any actual proof of items were only found in 1800BCE in China. Felt back then was primarily used to create horse blankets, clothing and gone along with jewelry. Fiber Art, mainly knitting became a main source of trade income for Europe which is how it is now well known among us humans. The cartoonish depiction of knitting also further spreads the knowledge of knitting. However as we know now, it is not the only art form of its type.



- Types of Fiber Art -

Crochet

This type of fiber Art involves using mainly a yarn and crochet hook to weave in between yarn and create complex patterns which results in a firm structure of intertwined yarn. A mistake could be incredible difficult to undo, and the process is incredibly time consuming as you need to carefully thread each yarn knot to the hook to create these patterns. I would recommend high amounts of patience when undertaking this!

Felting

Felting will be discussed more in the FELTING tab. Stay tuned!

Embroidery

Embroidery is much closerto traditional needlework, however instead of sewing clothes and adding laces, we use it on a cloth canvus to make art with those sewing lines! As disclosed, Emboridery requires cloth, string and a needle to perform.

Knitting

Knitting is by far the most well known type of Fiber Art. Knitting requires a pair of knitting needles and a ball of yarn. To even start knitting, one must create the first row of loops onto the needle.

Equipment

As with any art form, using the correct equipment is very important to even starting out! Felting mainly involves a Felting Board, a Felting Needle and Felt to start. Click on the icons below to find out more about these equipment!

The felting needle

Felting needles resemble real needles however often times these needles are given a base for us humans to easily hold with our entire hand as felting, compared to emboridery, requires constant stabbing of the wool that will become sturdier. If you could only use the force of your 2 fingers like an actual needle, we would run out of strength in minutes each session!

Some artists use felting needles that feature one or more needles bundled together. This reduces the chance of the needle breaking from relentless jabs into the wool as it increases the overall surface area of the place being stabbed. However, a felting needle with more needles also has its downsides. It is less precise due to covering a wider area and replacing the needles is more of a hassle compared to a felting needle with only one needle. Also ensure you have a plastic cap or anything to prevent the needle from breaking if you are carrying it around!

The felting board

The felting board is an especially crucial element to felting as this is how the stabbing magic happens. Polyurethane foam is used most of the time, serving the purpose of intertwining the delicate threads of fiber between two different wools together with its many tiny pores. The stabbing involved with the needle is simply to let the threads sink and combine into each other. An additional bonus, the sound produced from stabbing a felting board is incredibly satisfying! However, a felting board wears down overtime and becomes soft. This is when a change is required. While this depends on how much you felt everyday, I would simply recommend keeping spares on hand.

About Felt

Felt is a loosely condensed piece of fiber dyed in different colors. For this article, we will only focus on felt relating to specifically needle felting.

- A Guide to Felting -

After knowing about the equipment used for felting, we can start talking about how to actually felt!

Firstly, if you do not have the required equipment, head down to the bottom[put a hyprlink here] of the page for some recommendations for gear! To start felting, grab 2 pieces of different colored felt, put them on top of each other on the felting board, and use the neddle to stab the intersection points. Do this for about 3 minutes and if the two colored felts are stuck together, congratulations! You have learnt the basics of felting which will carry you forward for the rest of your journey. Try to stab the same combined felt you have now in the same spot and do so for 10 minutes. You will quickly notice that the felt has gotten harder in that region. This is the reason why felting takes a tremendous amount of time. If you were to attempt to make an object, hardening it to stop felt from being malleable could take days or even weeks for even a simple sphere. However, speaking of Spheres...

A common beginner exercise for felting includes make a sphere out of felt! I will run you through the general process of creating this in this section. Firstly, grab some felt and briefly morph it into something akin to a sphere. No need to be perfect - not that it even can be. Start by slightly stabbing once at every point to give the wool a shape it can maintain. Now, the time consuming part - stab the wool everywhere evenly until of your desired level of malleability. If you wanted a solid ball, stab it until its hard. If you want a soft base like for a bubble, only a bit of stabbing is required.

Frequently asked Questions

These are some frequently asked questions Ive seen relating to felting:

1. Can I mix the colors of felt together?
While not impossible, its highly not recommended when starting out as it requires quite perfect amounts of both wools that can intertwine with each other. Making it extremely limited in choice while being difficult to pull off.
2. What limitations does felt have?
Felt does not have any inherent limitations. You can make 2d art, 3d art or even clothing with it. However, do not expect a sword out of felt for example.
